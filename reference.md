# Pahari-Scroll reference: Kumaoni landscape, temples, painting grammar

Legend: [S] = from a cited source. [K] = general knowledge or my inference, not verified in this research. Treat all numeric proportions marked [K] as design suggestions, not measurements.

## 1. Jageshwar Dham (Almora, Kumaon)

Facts [S]:
- About 35-36 km NE of Almora, at ~1,870 m, in the Jataganga river valley among deodar (Cedrus deodara). Over 200 structural temples of cut stone spread over ~3.5 km; ASI protects ~151 pre-12th-c. monuments. Dated 7th-14th c. (some sources say to 18th c., Katyuri to Chand). (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jageshwar, https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/uttarakhand/almora/jageshwar-dham)
- Two main groups: Jageshwar group (~124 temples, inside one walled precinct) and Dandeshwar group (15 temples = 1 large + 14 small), about 1 km away. (https://inditales.com/jageshwar-shiva-temple-uttarakhand/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jageshwar)
- Style: North Indian Nagara. Simple square-plan sanctum with plain walls, tiered curvilinear tower (latina shikhara), capped by a cogged-disc amalaka, then kalasha (pot finial) / linga-like top. (Wikipedia; https://bhaktibharat.org/temples/famous-shiva-temples-yatra/jageshwar-dham-architecture-guide)
- Some temples have a later wooden canopy over the tower; Wikipedia: Temple 76's tower is covered by a wooden canopy, with the original amalaka beneath it. Locally the wooden roof is called "bijaura", said to be specific to the region and to give a Nepali/Tibetan-ish look, on the bigger temples. (https://inditales.com/jageshwar-shiva-temple-uttarakhand/)
- Variants: Dandeshwar has a distinctive receding-tier shikhara; Nav Durga, Chandika, Pushti Devi are valabhi (barrel-vault / wagon-roof) shikhara types; Kuber temple has an amalaka-sila disc on top; some towers carry a stacked series of shrinking amalaka discs (Wikipedia, bhaktibharat).
- Named temples: Jageshwar, Mahamrityunjaya, Dandeshwar, Chandi-ka, Kuber, Nav Durga / Nanda Devi, Navagraha, Surya, a pyramidal shrine, Kedareshwar, Pushti Devi, Baleshwar, Lakulisha.

Drawable inference [K]:
- Main temples: stout, roughly square footprint; height ~1.5-2.5x base width including tower; walls (jangha) ~40% of height, tower ~60%.
- Tower profile: slightly convex curve narrowing upward, vertical ribs/bands (bhumi-amalaka-like horizontal notches), then wide flat-ish amalaka disc (width ~0.5-0.6 of tower top), small kalasha, optional trishul/dhwaja pennant.
- Wooden bijaura variant: a broad overhanging hipped/pyramidal timber roof (eaves projecting ~0.3x base width on each side, dark brown, small gable ridge, sometimes short finial) sitting like a hat over the stone tower. This is the strongest "Kumaoni" silhouette: wide eaves + stone body below.
- Small dedri shrines: 0.2-0.4x the height of the main temples, same latina profile, packed shoulder to shoulder in rows, ~124 in the precinct. For generation, use clusters of 8-30 with random height jitter, plus 1-3 big temples.
- Stone: grey to blue-black, weathered, lichen-stained, dark under deodar shade (sources say "natural gray tones"). Palette suggestion: #6b6a66 base, #4a4a48 shade, #8a8578 lit, moss #5a6b4a.
- Layout: crowded in the valley bottom on one bank of the Jataganga, stream running past (small, rocky, shallow), temples on flat terraces; deodar trunks pierce between shrines; steep forested slopes rise behind.

## 2. Kumaoni vernacular houses

Facts [S] (https://nayalap.com/kumaoni-architecture/, https://www.kmvn.in/architecture, https://www.indianetzone.com/architecture_kumaon):
- Stone walls, mud floors, slate roofs ("pathal/pataal" stone slabs laid over logs and mud), grey-stone courtyards (patangan).
- Main entrance is the "kholi", wooden, carved with Ganesha and deities; flanking windows with painted/carved detail. Wood carving is called "likhai" (writing on wood).
- Aipan in red earth and ground rice is painted on porches and house fronts before Diwali.

Drawable inference [K]:
- Two storeys; ground floor for animals/storage, upper floor for living with a timber balcony; roof a simple gable, pitch ~25-35 deg, with slight eave overhang; slate colour dark grey with lighter slab edges (draw as horizontal scalloped rows).
- Walls: stone with whitewash/lime upper part, ochre-red geru band (~0.2-0.25 of wall height) along base; I am unsure how common this is, treat as stylisation. Door: dark wood, central, with rectangular carved panels and a lintel arch of motifs; 2-4 small wooden windows.
- Houses cluster in small hamlets of 3-12 on a ridge or mid-slope, stepped with the terrain, smoke from one chimney/roof gap.
- Terraces (seedhi khet): narrow contour-following steps, each riser ~1-3 m of dry-stone wall, tread width varying 3-15 m; on a drawn slope, 8-20 bands of alternating greens (wheat green, mustard yellow, bare brown) with thin grey wall lines. Curve bands following the hill contour.

## 3. Kangra / Pahari miniature visual grammar

Facts [S] (https://www.astaguru.com/blogs/kangra-paintings-the-soulful-art-of-himachal-pradesh-611, https://www.aiacaonline.org/craftkatha-pop-up/kangra-miniature, https://www.jrfadda.com/exams/ugc-net-notes/pahari-painting-himalayan-miniature-traditions/):
- Natural mineral and vegetable pigments (lapis for blue, malachite for green), gold and silver leaf; soft greens, blues, pinks, saffron, white highlights.
- Fine fluid line; backgrounds full of verdant trees, rivers, flowers, creepers, rivulets; many shades of green for foliage.
- Delicate floral borders around the composition.

Drawable inference / traditional knowledge [K]:
- Palette: indigo/ultramarine blue (#2f3f7a), ochre/yellow (#d8a038), malachite green (#4f8a5b) plus darker leaf green (#2f5a3a), vermilion/cinnabar (#d1402a), lamp black (#1c1a18), lead/shell white (#f3ecd8), pink (#e49ab0), gold (#c9a54a). Paper ground warm cream (#efe3c2).
- Hills: Kangra uses softly rounded, layered mounds in pastel greens with pale fading toward horizon; earlier Basohli/Guler/Pahari styles use stylised angular rocks with bright flat colour, rocks in grey-lilac-brown with darker outlines and tufts of grass. Use rounded mounds for foreground, faceted crags for far peaks.
- Trees: dense rounded or flame-shaped clumps of dark green with lighter outlined leaf clusters; slender cypress/cedar spires; mango with round crown; banana with long drooping fronds; trunks thin, dark, often leaning; blossoming creepers around them. Draw each as a repeated leaf-blob stamp with a pale highlight edge.
- Skies: flat colour (often deep blue, or a light band) with a narrow gold or red horizon strip; monsoon variant with swirling dark clouds and white lightning zigzag. Low horizon line or none: figures and architecture stacked.
- Perspective: flat, tiered; buildings seen frontal/axonometric, distant elements stacked above nearer ones, not shrunk much.
- Border (hashiya): red (sometimes blue) outer band of ~3-6% of width, thin black and white inner rules, sometimes floral scroll in the margin; a cream inner line then the picture.

## 4. Aipan

Facts [S] (https://memeraki.com/blogs/news/aipan-art-the-uttaranchal-legacy, https://esamskriti.com/e/Culture/Indian-Art/About-AIPAN-Art-Kumaon,-Uttarakhand-1.aspx, https://colorsofuttarakhand.com/?p=367):
- Ground of wet red ochre/geru mud; design in white rice paste ("bisvar") drawn by women with fingers.
- Motifs: lotus, swastika, conch, goddess footprints (devi ke pair), creepers, floral patterns, geometric mandalas radiating from a central dot; designs begin and end with a dot.
Border use [K]: red strip #9a3b1f, white lines #f6efe0, 2-3 parallel rules with repeated units: dot-pairs, zigzag, chain of small lotuses, double-footprint pairs every ~10 units, conch glyph at corners, swastika/four-petal cross in corner squares. Dot grids on a 1:1 lattice with diamonds between.

## 5. Other scrollable landscape elements

Sources are thin for this section (partly https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jageshwar for deodar and Jataganga; the rest is my knowledge [K]).
- Deodar (Himalayan cedar): tall, conical with drooping tiered branches, dark blue-green. Mid-slope forest (1,600-2,500 m).
- Chir pine: lower slopes, lighter yellow-green, long needles, sparse crowns, reddish bark; rhododendron (burans): small trees with crimson flowers (Mar-Apr), use vermilion dots in the canopy; Kumaoni oak (banj) dense, rounded and dark; kafal: small tree with red berries (summer).
- Streams: Jataganga as a narrow, braided, stony stream; simple stone slab or log footbridges (wooden bridges plausible, not verified); suspension bridges exist in region.
- Religious markers (Hindu, not Buddhist): NO prayer flags (those belong to Bhutia/Tibetan areas, e.g. higher Pithoragarh, Johar). Use temple bells hung at doorways, trishul (iron trident) planted by shrines, dhwaja (saffron triangular flags on pole), small roadside stone shrines (many near Jageshwar approach), red-cloth-tied trees, aipan on thresholds.
- Horizon: Nanda Devi / Panchachuli snow ranges, white-lilac jagged, low contrast, pale with haze. Layered ridges fade to blue/grey; mist/cloud bands in valleys between ridges (flat white strips as in Shan Shui).
- Life: pack mules in a line along a switchback path, shepherds (anwal) with sheep or goats, women carrying grass bundles (doko baskets, my recollection), cattle on terraces.

## Implementation hints (for {Shan, Shui}-style)
- Layers back to front: sky band, far snow range, 3-4 fading ridge bands with mist strips, mid hills with terraces/hamlets, near ridge with deodar and temple cluster, foreground ground with stream.
- Use the hashiya + aipan frame fixed on screen while the interior scrolls.
- Temple cluster as a recurring "chunk" type every ~3-5 screen widths; Kumaoni hamlet and terraced hills as the other chunks.

## Observed from reference photos (2026-10-01, Commons: Complete_pic_of_Jageshwar_temple.jpg, Jageshwar_(6133300647).jpg)

### Jageshwar shikhara (verified visually)
- Silhouette: tall tapering trapezoid, height ≈ 2.0–2.5× base width. Sides are gently concave (curvilinear), not straight.
- Surface: strong horizontal banding — stacked ribbed courses every ~1/20 of height. This banding is the single most recognisable texture; keep it as repeated thin horizontal lines.
- Central vertical projection (lata) on each face → stepped plan, so the outline has 2–3 shallow steps per side.
- Amalaka: oversized ribbed cog-disc, WIDER than the top of the tower (≈ 0.9–1.1× the tower's base width at the top where the tower has narrowed to ~0.5×). Looks like a fat mushroom cap. Draw as an ellipse with 12–16 vertical ribs.
- Kalasha: small pot-shaped knob on the amalaka, ≈ 0.15× amalaka width.
- Bijaura (wooden canopy): a flat square roof on four slender posts standing OVER the amalaka of the main temple only. Painted blue-green in the photo. One per cluster, on the tallest tower.
- Small dedri shrines: same proportions scaled to 0.25–0.4× of the main tower, packed tightly, often overlapping. A cluster reads as 8–20 towers of varied heights, main tower off-centre.
- Stone colour: olive-brown-grey, darker in recesses. Flat Kangra treatment: #6b6248 fills, #3c3626 band lines.
- Setting: towers sit on a flat stone plinth; low railings; deodar and chir pine on the hill behind; whitewashed village houses on the slope behind.

### Kumaoni house (verified visually)
- Two storeys, whitewashed walls with ochre/pink (geru) lower walls or whole walls on older houses.
- Roof: low-pitch gable, slate "pathal" shingles (grey, irregular courses) — draw as grey trapezoid with 4–6 wavy horizontal rows. Newer ones use red clay tile or corrugated tin; stick to slate for the scroll.
- Wooden balcony with X-lattice railing on upper floor; windows with dark wood frames, sometimes painted blue doors.
- Houses sit on stepped stone retaining walls (dry-stone, visible courses), with steps zig-zagging between levels.
- Deodar directly behind: tall, layered horizontal branch tiers, dark blue-green (#2f4a3a).
